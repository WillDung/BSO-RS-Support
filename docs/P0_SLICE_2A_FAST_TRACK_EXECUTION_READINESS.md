## 3. Flow method and preservation

Microsoft documents working with solution-aware cloud flows as Dataverse `workflow` records and shows a general PATCH, but its example uses `If-Match: *`. That wildcard is not an exact-version concurrency guard. The historical SDK `RowVersion` and `versionnumber` are not, by themselves, proof of an HTTP ETag or that a specific `workflow.clientdata` update honors an `If-Match` precondition. The reviewed MCP, PAC, and management-connector surfaces did not demonstrate the required conditional single-Flow update either. This is a lack of demonstrated proof, not a claim that the platform cannot support it.

The offline comparison found exactly **26** changed `runtimeConfiguration.secureData` locations: one trigger and 25 actions. Removing only these privacy changes yields the same canonical pre-hardening definition, including the trigger, concurrency, connections, actions, expressions, retries, and terminal writes. The resulting local source protects the trigger and **33** actions, including `Set_location_changed` outside the contract's 32-action inventory. This is not a fresh live Flow read or proof that the platform retains or enforces the settings.

Before any proposed write, independently confirm the previously approved caller and target, resolve or explicitly except the Stage A drift, prove an applicable supported version-conditional update, and re-read the exact OFF Flow, membership, bindings, and full definition. A separately authorized Gate 2 must compare the complete returned definition, identity, state, membership, bindings, and every intended privacy setting. Separate synthetic runtime privacy tests are needed for actual run-history suppression. No action-level combination is presumed supported merely because it serializes locally.

## 5. Authorization, rollback, and uncertain outcomes

The present authorization covers offline preparation and bounded read-only verification only. Console update, publication, browser observation, Flow update, rollback, guard registration, configuration values, activation, synthetic runtime, mailbox access, and security acceptance are distinct scopes. Before a future Console change, capture separate recoverable published (`P0`) and current/unpublished (`C0`) HTML bytes and verify both. The normal plan stops unless `P0 = C0` and the fresh baselines match. If an acknowledged update is verified but publication was not dispatched, a separately approved rollback would conditionally restore `C0` without publishing. If both update and publication were definitely verified and `P0 = C0`, a separately approved rollback could conditionally restore `P0` and publish only that HTML, followed by independent layer verification. Different pre-existing layers require a separately reviewed multi-step recovery; one publication cannot be assumed to restore both.

For either resource or Flow operations, a timeout, lost response, unexpected result, failed precondition, concurrent drift, or incomplete readback is **STOP**. Preserve the journal and undertake only separately authorized read-only reconciliation; do not automatically retry, publish, or restore. Rollback itself needs fresh identity and state checks, proven concurrency protection, a new approval, and independent readback.

Normal-app served-byte verification requires separately approved browser observation because startup may make non-GET platform requests. Do not substitute direct resource fetching, rewrite URLs, clear caches, unregister service workers, or deliberately invoke business actions. A missing served layer remains unverified.
